A longer explanation of the whole journey

These two stories tie the course together. You can also select an individual stage to see its definitions.

Start with a concrete story

First story: Take ordinary integers such as 2, 7 and 12. You can place them in a set, use a rule to give each a remainder, and group numbers with the same remainder. This becomes arithmetic with remainder-groups. Under special conditions, that arithmetic is a field.

Second story: A field gives us numbers that can scale vectors. From vectors we build combinations, ask how much we can reach (span), and ask whether we have extras (independence). Both tests together produce a basis; counting a basis gives dimension.

Choose items→Group them→Do arithmetic→Scale vectors→Build + remove extras→Count directions

This is a teaching route, not a claim that every mathematical topic can only be defined using the one before it.

When you're stuck: six steps

Work through the steps in order. Tick each one as you go. If you've been stuck for more than a few minutes, go back to step 3 and ask which condition you haven't checked.

Questions to ask yourself

  1. What exactly is given, and what do I have to show or find?
  2. Which definition applies, and what are its conditions?
  3. What does this sentence mean in ordinary words?
  4. Can I test a small case to see what is happening?
  5. Am I assuming a picture proves a general claim?
